# Still & Cask: a gin-to-whisky distillery game

> **Status: idea, not started** (Andrew's idea, 1 October 2026). A separate game from Debuggit,
> sharing no saves, like `bbq-idle-concept.md`. The name is a placeholder, and every number here
> is a first guess.

The pitch: you run a craft distillery. You start by distilling and selling **gin**, which is
quick to make and sell, to pay the bills, and work your way up to **whisky**, which ties up money
for years in barrels before it earns anything. Ageing is already an idle-game timer: you fill a
cask and come back to it.

## What the player does

- **Buy the inputs.** Grain (and botanicals for gin), power to run the still, and barrels, each
  with its own price that moves.
- **Distil.** Gin is the starter product: distil, flavour with botanicals and sell straight
  away, so it's the steady cash early on. Whisky is distilled the same way but then has to age.
- **Decide how long to age, and in what.** Each batch of whisky goes into a barrel type you
  choose, such as **virgin oak**, **ex-bourbon**, **sherry** or **port**, for as long as you
  choose. Longer ageing is worth more, but the cask ties up money and space, and some spirit
  evaporates every year (the "angels' share").
- **Sell into a changing market.** Prices move, and so does **public taste**: one year sherry
  casks are in fashion, then virgin oak, then something else. Reading where taste is heading,
  years before a cask is ready, is the core decision.

## Progression

1. **Gin start-up:** a small still, buying in grain and botanicals, selling gin locally.
2. **First whisky casks:** gin profits fund grain and barrels for whisky that won't sell for a
   while; the tension is how much to lay down versus keeping cash on hand.
3. **A warehouse of casks:** a range of ages and barrel types, so there's always something ready
   to sell whichever way taste moves.
4. Later: bigger stills, your own barrels or grain, staff, a brand and reputation, exports.

## Ideas from Debuggit Ltd that carry over

- Market moves every so many hours (Ltd's hiring market) become grain, power and barrel prices.
- Running costs drawn every second, offline too (payroll and rent) become power and warehouse
  costs.
- A news feed (planned for Ltd's AI models) could announce shifts in public taste ahead of time.

## Open questions

- How fast does time run? Real years are too slow, so a whisky year needs to be minutes or
  hours of play.
- Is there an active layer, like Ltd's desk jobs (e.g. tasting or blending decisions)?
- Do other spirits (rum, vodka, brandy) come in, or is it gin then whisky?
- How does taste change: random, cyclical, or announced in advance?
